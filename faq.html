Frequently Asked Questions

About Taniti

Taniti is a small, tropical island in the Pacific. While the island has an area of less than 500 square miles, the terrain is varied and includes both sandy and rocky beaches, a small but safe harbor, lush tropical rainforests, and a mountainous interior that includes a small, active volcano. Taniti has an indigenous population of about 20,000. Until a recent increase in tourism, most the Tanitian economy was dominated by fishing or agriculture.

What is the local currency and what payment methods are accepted?

Taniti uses the U.S. dollar as its primary currency, but many local businesses will also accept euros and yen. Several banks across the island facilitate currency exchange, and major credit cards are widely accepted by businesses.

What are the local laws regarding alcohol sales and the drinking age?

The legal drinking age on Taniti is 18; however, it is worth noting that this drinking age is not strictly enforced. Please be aware that alcohol is not allowed to be served or sold anywhere on the island between the hours of midnight and 9:00 a.m.

Is English widely spoken on the island?

Many younger Tanitians speak fluent English. However, very little English is spoken in rural areas, especially by the older residents of the island.

What type of electrical outlets are used on Taniti?

Power outlets on the island are 120 volts, which is exactly the same standard voltage and plug style used in the United States.

What medical facilities are available if I get sick or injured?

There is one central hospital and several clinics operating on the island. For international travelers' convenience, the hospital has many multilingual employees on staff.

Is Taniti safe for tourists?

Violent crime is very rare on Taniti. However, as tourism to our beautiful island increases, there are more frequent reports of pickpocketing and other petty crimes. We recommend keeping an eye on your belongings in crowded spaces.

Do tourist attractions close for national holidays?

Yes. Taniti enjoys a large number of national holidays. Many tourist attractions and local restaurants will completely close down on these holidays, so visitors should plan their trip schedules accordingly.
